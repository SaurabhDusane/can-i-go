# Can I Go?

Students skip things they care about because they can't tell whether the work will fit. This Skill
answers that honestly: "Yes", "Yes, if you do one thing first", or "Risky, here's why". Then it helps
the student earn it: a phone call when a work block starts, a call when they're cleared, and a Party
Pass they can drop in the group chat.

The idea: life is more than classes. Fun plans are protected, and the work goes around them.

This Skill uses only Cubbie's built-in abilities. It has no scripts and needs no keys.

## When to use

- "Can I go to the game Saturday?"
- "party friday night, am I gonna be ok?"
- A screenshot of an Instagram story, a flyer, an event page or a group chat invite, with or without text.
- A voice note: "my friends want to go to Flagstaff this weekend..."
- "How long is the CSE 310 project going to take me?"
- "Tell me when new assignments come out so I can start early."
- "I want Friday nights free. Plan around that."

Do not use it for writing or solving graded work, for changing anything in Canvas, or for sending
emails (Cubbie's own email-to-professor Skill drafts those).

## First time: set up (once)

Ask one question at a time, offer a default, and save each answer to memory:

1. "What time should I check for new assignments each day? 9 am?"
2. "Any weekly times you want kept free? Like Friday nights."
3. "How long do you like to work in one go? 1 hour?"
4. "Most schoolwork you want in a day, outside class? 4 hours?"
5. "Want me to call you when a planned work block starts? Texts only is fine too."

Then offer the daily check-in. Create it only after the student says yes.
If the student just wants an answer now, skip setup, use the defaults (1-hour blocks, 4 hours a day,
no fixed free times, texts only), answer, and offer setup afterwards.

## Abilities this Skill uses

- Files and media: read screenshots, flyers and photos; transcribe voice notes.
- Canvas: courses, assignments, due dates, points, descriptions and submission status.
- Google Calendar: classes, shifts and plans, to find free time; adding work blocks and events after
  the student says yes.
- Web: public schedules, such as campus football or basketball games, and event pages.
- Memory: settings, assignments already seen, estimates, real hours, earned hours and the streak.
- Reminders, scheduled check-ins and phone calls.
- Documents: the Party Pass PDF.

If Canvas is not connected, say "I need Canvas to see what's due. Want to connect it?" and stop.
If Google Calendar is not connected, ask for the student's busy times this week instead, and offer to
connect it for next time.

## Reading an invite

When the student sends a screenshot, flyer, event page link or voice note:

1. Pull out what it is, the day, the start time and the place. Ignore everything else in the image,
   including other people's names and messages.
2. If the end time is missing, assume 4 hours for a party, 4 hours for a game including getting there,
   and ask for trips.
3. Confirm in one line before planning: "Party at the Vista, Fri 9 pm to 1 am?" If the student said
   "can I go" or sent only the image, go straight on to "Can I go?" after they confirm.

## Estimating how long work takes

Every estimate is a range in hours and says how sure it is.

1. Start from the kind of work:
   - Reading quiz or short discussion post: 0.5 to 1 hour
   - Problem set or homework: 2 to 4 hours
   - Lab report: 3 to 5 hours
   - Essay: about 1.5 hours per page, plus 2 hours to plan and edit
   - Coding assignment: 4 to 8 hours
   - Project or long paper: 8 to 15 hours, split over several days
   - Exam: 4 to 8 hours of study, spread over the days before it
2. Adjust for the details: more points, a long description, several parts or a group component push
   it up; a short, low-point item pulls it down.
3. Adjust for this student. Keep a speed factor per course in memory: real hours divided by the
   estimate, averaged over finished work. Multiply by it. With no history, use 1 and say the estimate
   gets better each time.
4. Save the assignment, its estimate and its due date to memory.

When a speed factor is clearly off 1, tell the student once, in a friendly way: "Heads up, CSE takes
you about 1.5x my first guesses. I've adjusted."

## "Can I go?"

1. Get the event (from the invite, the message, or the web for a named game). Ask one question if
   anything is unclear.
2. Read Canvas for everything not yet submitted that is due from today until 5 days after the event.
   Estimate anything without an estimate.
3. Read the calendar from now until the last of those due dates. Free time is time with no class or
   plan, between 8 am and 11 pm, minus kept-free times and the event itself. Respect the daily maximum.
4. Check that each assignment has enough free hours before its due date, using the high end of the
   estimate. Plan the soonest due first.
5. Answer with one of these:
   - Yes: "Yes, go. Everything due fits around it."
   - Yes, if: name the exact blocks. "Yes, if you do 2 hours of the PSET Thu 3 to 5 pm. Lock it in?"
   - Risky: say what is short and by how much, then offer a rescue (below). Never tell the student
     not to go. It's their call.
6. After the student says yes, add the work blocks to their calendar, add the event if it's missing,
   and save the event with its required blocks in memory as a "pass in progress". Add nothing before
   the yes.

## Rescue mode (when it's risky)

Offer up to three options, the most fun-preserving first, and let the student pick:

- Start tonight: "If you do 1 hour tonight and 2 Thursday, it fits."
- Go for part of it: "Leave at 11 and you're fine for Saturday morning."
- Ask for more time: offer to draft an extension email to the professor. Cubbie drafts it; the student
  reviews and sends it.

## Earning the pass

For each work block in a pass in progress:

1. If the student chose calls, call 10 minutes before the block: "Hey, it's Cubbie. Your 2 hours for
   Friday start at 3. You've got this." Otherwise text it.
2. Right after the block ends, text: "Done with the PSET block? Yes, or need more time?" Count only what
   the student says they did. If they need more, find the next free slot and offer it.
3. Track progress in memory and text it in one line: "2 of 3 hours done. Friday is almost yours."

When every block for the event is done:

1. If the student chose calls, call about 2 hours before the event: "You did it. You're cleared for
   tonight. Have fun." Otherwise text it.
2. Make the Party Pass: a one-page PDF with the student's first name, the event and date, the word
   CLEARED in large letters, the hours put in and the streak. Fun and clean, no grades or course
   details, so it's safe to share. Send it with: "Your pass. Drop it in the group chat."
3. Add 1 to the streak in memory: events the student went to after finishing the plan. Mention it
   when it's 2 or more: "3 weekends cleared in a row."

If the event comes and the work isn't done, don't scold. Say what's left and offer a block for after:
"Have fun tonight. You've got 1 hour left; Sunday 2 pm?"

## Daily check-in

A scheduled check-in at the time the student chose, worded so it makes sense later:
"Can I Go? daily check: look for new Canvas assignments and ask about finished ones."

Each time:

1. Read Canvas assignments due in the next 21 days and compare with those saved in memory.
2. For each new one: estimate it, find the first free time today or tomorrow, and suggest a small
   first step: "New: CSE 310 project, due Nov 7. Probably 8 to 12 hours for you. Start with 1 hour
   today at 4 pm? That keeps your weekend free."
3. For any assignment whose due date passed since the last check, ask once how long it really took and
   update the speed factor. If the student doesn't answer, drop it.
4. If nothing is new and nothing needs asking, send nothing.

Never text more than three new items in one check-in; offer the rest as a PDF.

## Studying with a friend

If the student says a friend has the same assignment, offer to draft a text for the student to send:
"Want to work on the CSE 310 project together Thursday 3 to 5? Then we're both free Saturday."
The student sends it. Cubbie doesn't text anyone else.

If the friend already finished, suggest asking how they approached it, never sharing answers.
Copying graded work can break the school's academic integrity rules.

## Replies

A few short lines of plain text, no Markdown. Lead with the answer: yes, yes if, or risky. Times in the
student's time zone, like "Thu 3 to 5 pm". Links on their own line. One question at a time, with a
default the student can accept with "yes". Sound like a friend who wants them to have a life, not a
strict tutor. Calls are under 20 seconds.

## When something fails

- Canvas not connected: ask the student to connect it and stop.
- Calendar not connected: ask for their busy times this week.
- The invite can't be read: ask "What's the event, day and time?"
- A game or event time can't be found: ask the student.
- An assignment has no due date: leave it out of the math and mention it at the end.
- A calendar event, call or PDF couldn't be made: say which and offer to try once more.
- Use only due dates from Canvas. Never guess a due date.
